Understanding Touchline tiers

On Division I pages Touchline shows the shooting and scoring tier for men's forwards and midfielders, and on team pages the team defense badge. Tiers highlight standout box-score performance — not overall player ability.

Percentages are within one season's eligible forwards and midfielders, compared separately, or the division's teams. About 80% of eligible players hold no tier, and about 80% of teams hold no badge.

HOW EACH POSITION IS MARKED

  • Midfielders: Tier 1 (the top 5%) or Tier 2 (the next 15%).
  • Forwards: one tier, the top 20%. A forward's scoring swings more from match to match than a midfielder's, so Touchline marks the top fifth rather than splitting it, the same way on every level.
  • Defenders and goalkeepers: no player tier. A regular's sheet prints the team's defensive figures, and the team defense badge goes to the team.

No tier is not a bad mark.

HOW THE TIER IS SET

Step for step as on every level: non-penalty goals and shots per 90, each adjusted for the defense faced and for home and away; blended with the player's previous season at the same program when that season counts, and pulled toward the group average; then the cut inside each group, ties included. A player is eligible with 900 minutes this season, or 600 this season and 600 the season before.

Two weightings are fitted on Division I's own seasons, by the same method on every level:

  • Threat = 0.207 × goals + 0.0837 × shots, per 90 (one goal counts as 2.47 shots).
  • One season counts: 0.80 × this season + 0.20 × group average.
  • Two seasons count: 0.60 × this season + 0.30 × previous + 0.10 × group average.

The full method, with worked examples, is on the Division II tiers page.

HOW THE BADGE IS SET

Over a team's complete matches (at least 12), Touchline compares the goals it conceded with what its opponents usually score: record = ln(opponents' usual ÷ goals conceded). The top fifth of the division's teams that season, ties included, hold the badge. Keeping opponents from shooting counts as much as stopping their shots, so it credits the whole team's defending, from the front line to the goalkeeper. It is one season's, and is not averaged with earlier seasons.

WHEN TIERS ARE SET

Tiers are calculated once a season, in November, and are not moved afterward. The minutes printed beside a tier are the minutes counted through that day. Tiers for the seasons before 2026 were worked out in November 2026 by the same rule, from every match of those seasons, postseason included. The first season with tiers is 2022, and nothing before it is counted: a 2022 forward or midfielder is judged on that season alone.
