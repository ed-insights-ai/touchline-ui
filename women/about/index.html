TOUCHLINE · NCAA DIVISION II WOMEN'S SOCCER

About & method

Touchline is a season journal for NCAA Division II women's soccer, following 21 conferences, listed below. It is independent and unofficial: it has no connection to any conference, program or governing body. Everything on it comes from the programs' own published pages — and what they did not publish is shown as what it is.

WHAT THE WORDS MEAN

PLAYED

A match marked final whose score was published. Touchline counts nothing it cannot show.

A RECORD, W–D–L

Wins, draws and losses, always in that order: 15–3–2 is fifteen wins, three draws and two losses. A conference table adds points, three for a win and one for a draw, and counts only matches between its own members.

SETTLED ON PENALTIES

A match level at the end of play and decided by a shootout counts as a draw in every record and table. The score stays the drawn one, the shootout is printed beside it — 1–1 (4–3 pens) — and it decides only who goes through.

ROUND CHIPS

GNAC FINAL NCAA R3 A postseason match wears its tournament and round: outlined for a conference tournament, filled for the NCAA's. R1, R2 and so on number the rounds from the first; QF, SF and FINAL are the quarterfinal, semifinal and final. A tie whose round the published results cannot place wears its tournament alone. In a season-strip cell too narrow for the whole chip, the round stands alone.

WHERE A SEASON STANDS
BEFORE THE TABLE
No conference result yet. The table ranks every match played, against anyone, and says so.
CONFERENCE PLAY UNDER WAY
The conference has a league result, and the table is the conference table.
CONFERENCE TOURNAMENT UNDER WAY
The conference tournament has a collected match and its final is still to come. The regular-season table is final.
TOURNAMENT DECIDED
The tournament's final has been played. It says the tournament is over, not who won it: where no published result names a winner, Touchline names none.
SCORE GAP

A match a program's site marked finished without ever publishing a score. The silence is the site's own; it stays on the record, named, and every collect asks again.

BOX SCORE GAP

A match whose result Touchline holds but whose detail — the box score behind it — the collector could not reach. Never a friendly, never a score gap: a box score gap is the one absence nothing else on the page names.

A SHARED MATCH

A match between programs in two of the conferences Touchline follows belongs to both. It appears in each conference's own accounting, and the division counts it once — so adding the conference figures together gives a larger number than the division's, and neither of them is wrong. When only one of the two programs has posted the score, Touchline takes it and says so: a final on one side only, named on the match page and in each conference's accounting until the other page catches up.

V, A NEUTRAL SITE

A small “v” beside a score, or between two names where “at” would stand: the published schedule lists the match at a neutral site. Home and away stay as the schedule printed them, so a Match Centre may still name one side the home side.

FRIENDLY

A match played outside the record everywhere: no wins, no goals, no counts. Named where it appears, counted nowhere. The 2026 collection is the first to mark them, so on earlier seasons Touchline cannot tell a friendly from a match that counted — and does not guess which was which.

LEAGUE MATCH

A match between two members of the same conference that counts toward its table. Wherever one appears — on the season line, in the week's docket, on the matches page — it wears the site's purple, which means nothing else.

STILL LISTED AS SCHEDULED

A date that has passed while the program's schedule still calls the match scheduled: no result, no postponement, no note. A different absence from a score gap, and counted apart from it.

THE TABLE BEFORE CONFERENCE PLAY

Until the first conference result, the table ranks every match a member has played, against anyone, and says so. From that result on it is the conference table, with the record across all matches kept beneath each side.

MATCHWEEK

A Monday-to-Sunday week with at least one match in it. The season line counts them, and the week's docket prints the same days.

HOW TO READ THE EVIDENCE

Where a page has to say how far a figure stands from the page it came from — the championship's bracket and its tie sheets — it carries one of two marks.

OBSERVED ●●●
Published. A score, a date or a name exactly as a program's own page printed it; Touchline copied it and computed nothing.
DERIVED ●●○
Worked out exactly from published values — which round a tie was, who went through — and shown with what it was worked out from. Anyone holding the same pages gets the same answer. Never an estimate.

WHAT IS CHECKED

Every figure on this site is recomputed from the published pages it came from before it is printed. A sentence that fails the recount is dropped, not softened, and every number in prose answers to a figure that was checked. Where a sentence carries figures, it opens onto them: the numbers it rests on, and the layer they were read from.

WHERE IT COMES FROM

A collector reads each program's own schedule, roster, statistics and box score pages, once a day, and writes what it finds to a file. This site is built from those files and one hand-kept reference — each program's nickname and city, the only facts here no collector produced. No estimates, no third-party feeds.

The home page's map draws the lower 48 states. Chaminade, Hawai'i Hilo and Hawai'i Pacific are off this map: their towns lie outside the frame it draws.
