How tiers work

A tier marks one thing a season shows clearly in the published box scores. Forwards and midfielders can hold a tier for their own shooting and scoring. Defenders and goalkeepers can share a tier that belongs to their team's defending. Most players hold no tier, and no tier is not a bad mark.

WHAT A TIER IS, AND IS NOT

A tier is a distinction, worked out by a published rule from the box scores, and shown with what it rests on: minutes, appearances, the seasons counted and class year.

It is not a rating, and there is no score, ranking or order inside a tier. It is not a prediction. It describes one part of the game the record can see; players matter in many ways it cannot.

WHO CAN HOLD ONE

Positions Forwards and midfielders Defenders and goalkeepers
Tiers Tier 1, the top 5% of eligible players in the position group. Tier 2, the next 15%. One tier: the top fifth of eligible players.
Eligible 900 minutes this season, or 600 in each of the last two as a forward or midfielder at the same programme. Two or three seasons in a row as a regular at the same programme.
Seasons counted This one, and the one before if it had 600 minutes as a forward or midfielder. Each season in a row as a regular, up to three.

A defender is a regular with 900 minutes and 70% of the team's minutes in a season; a goalkeeper, 600 minutes and 50%. Men and women are compared separately.

Positions come from the roster. Where it lists two, the one the box scores show most, if they show it at least three times; otherwise the first listed. Where it lists none, the box scores.

Minutes come from the box scores. Where a programme's box scores do not print minutes for every match, the season total the programme publishes is used, or the box-score minutes if they are higher. Where it publishes no total, the box-score minutes are used if at least half the matches print them. So the minutes beside a tier can differ from the season total on the same sheet. Where they do, the sheet says where the tier's count comes from.

SHOOTING AND SCORING

A player's own goals and shots, penalties left out, for every 90 minutes played. Against a defence that gives up few, a goal or a shot counts for a little more; against one that gives up many, a little less. Home and away are allowed for.

A goal counts for between two and a half and a little over three shots. Shots count because they say more about the goals to come than past goals do. With two seasons counted, this one weighs most. Assists and the pass before a shot are not counted.

TEAM DEFENCE

This tier belongs to the team. A box score says who scored, not who won a tackle, so Touchline does not split a team's defending between its players.

Each season the teams fall into three equal groups by shots faced a match: light, typical and heavy workload. A team is compared only with its own group, so a defence is not rewarded for being rarely tested. Inside the group, a season is measured by the goals given up against the number the opponents usually score. A player's record is that, averaged over the seasons counted, and the top fifth hold the tier. Players of one team with the same seasons always share a result.

THE TEAM FIGURES

Every regular defender's and goalkeeper's sheet shows three, tier or no tier.

  1. Shots faced a match, with the workload group. Which teams yours is compared with.
  2. Shots faced that went in. The share that ended in the net.
  3. Goals given up, beside the number the opponents usually score. What those opponents score in a typical match, added up over the matches played. This is the figure the tier compares.

The first two sit beside the division's figure. The third reads as one sentence, such as “Gave up 24 goals where its opponents usually score 31.” A heavy workload team with the tier may have given up more goals than a light workload team without it; the figures show it plainly.

WHAT A SHEET SAYS

Every player at a Division II programme Touchline follows, with a box score line, has one of these.

  • A tier, with its kind, such as Tier 2 · Shooting and scoring.
  • Eligible, no tier. Compared, and no tier this season. About four in five compared players.
  • Needs more minutes. Nothing else is implied.
  • Needs a second season. A regular defender or goalkeeper this season, but not here the season before.
  • Incomplete records. The record cannot be compared fairly, and the sheet says why: minutes not published for enough matches, no position on the roster or in the box scores, a name that could not be matched from one match to the next, or, for defenders and goalkeepers, too few of the team's matches collected.

WHAT TOUCHLINE CANNOT SEE

Where someone played on the field. One player's defending. Seasons at another programme. And why a player did not play: a box score lists who played, not who was injured or rested, and Touchline never states or implies a reason.

HOW FIRM A TIER IS

Small differences can move a player near the line. Position matters too: a player listed in another position group is compared with a different set of players.

To test a tier, Touchline splits a season's matches into two random halves and sets the tiers on each. Of Tier 1 players on one half, about eight or nine in ten hold a tier on the other; among men's forwards, seven in ten. Of Tier 2 players, about half do. Luck alone would give two in ten. A full season is steadier than half of one.

Team defence is less firm. Of players holding it on one half, about four in ten hold it on the other. Of holders who are regulars again the next season, about seven in ten hold it again, partly because the seasons counted overlap.

WHEN TIERS ARE SET

Once a season, in November, before the postseason honours are announced, and not moved after. The minutes beside a tier are the minutes to that day. Tiers for the seasons before 2026 were worked out in November 2026 by the same rule, from every match of those seasons, postseason included. The first season with tiers is 2022, and nothing before it is counted: no 2022 defender or goalkeeper could hold the team defence tier, and a 2022 forward or midfielder is judged on that season alone.

DIVISION II ONLY

Tiers cover Division II men's and women's soccer at the programmes Touchline follows. Division I pages show none.
