TOUCHLINE · NCAA DIVISION II

How tiers work

A tier is a top-of-group label calculated by a fixed formula from published box scores. Touchline runs two separate tracks: Shooting and scoring for forwards and midfielders (individual non-penalty goals and shots per 90 minutes, adjusted for opponents), and Team defence for defenders and goalkeepers (how many goals their team prevented relative to similar-workload teams over two or three seasons). Four in five eligible players hold no tier, and holding no tier is not a negative mark.

WHAT A TIER IS, AND IS NOT

Every tier is worked out by the same published math from the box scores and shown alongside the record it rests on: minutes, appearances, seasons counted and class year.

It is not an overall player rating, a ranking or a prediction. Inside a tier there is no #1 and no #50 — the intermediate scores used to draw the cut lines are discarded and never printed. A tier only describes the part of the game that box scores record; players help their team in many ways a box score never sees.

ELIGIBILITY, POSITIONS AND MINUTES

Separate pools

Men and women are always modelled and compared separately. In shooting and scoring, forwards are compared only with forwards and midfielders only with midfielders, because forwards naturally take more shots and score more goals. In team defence, eligible defenders and goalkeepers are pooled together because every player carries their team's defensive score.

What counts as a defensive regular

To count a season toward team defence, a player must clear both a minutes floor and a share of the team's recorded minutes (minutes ÷ (team matches × 90)):

  • Defender: at least 900 minutes and at least 70% of the team's minutes.
  • Goalkeeper: at least 600 minutes and at least 50% of the team's minutes.

The team must also have at least 12 collected matches with complete goal and shot counts in each counted season.

How position is chosen

Position comes from the programme's roster. If a roster lists two positions (such as “F/M”), Touchline checks the player's box-score lines that season: if one of those listed positions appears most often and at least three times, that one is used; otherwise the roster's primary listing stands. If the roster gives no position, Touchline uses the most common position printed in the player's box scores (ties go to the first seen in date order).

How minutes are counted

Not every programme prints player minutes in every match box score. Touchline picks a player's minutes in this order:

  1. All box scores print minutes: Touchline sums the minutes from the match box scores.
  2. Some box scores omit minutes, but the programme publishes a season total: Touchline uses the season total from the programme's stats page — or the box-score sum if it is higher (a printed minute was definitely played). For goalkeepers, both the players table and the goalkeeping table are checked and the higher total is used.
  3. Some box scores omit minutes and no season total is published: Touchline uses the box-score sum as long as at least half of the team's matches print minutes. If a player falls short of eligibility on printed minutes, Touchline checks whether adding 90 minutes for each unprinted match they appeared in could lift them over the line; if it could, the player is marked Incomplete records rather than Needs more minutes.

Because the stats page and the box scores do not always match — and because tiers freeze once a season — the minutes beside a tier can differ from the season total elsewhere on the same player sheet. When they differ, the sheet states where the tier's count came from.

STEP 1 FOR BOTH TRACKS: OPPONENT STRENGTH AND HOME FIELD

Before scoring any player or defence, Touchline measures how strong every team's attack and defence were and how much home field mattered. That way, a goal scored away against a stingy defence counts for more than a goal scored at home against a leaky one — and a defence facing high-scoring opponents gets credit for the schedule it played.

Touchline fits two identical multiplicative models across all collected seasons (men and women separately): one for goals and one for shots. Each team-season receives an attack factor (att) and a defence factor (dfn), centred around 1.0 for an average team:

Expected in a match = μ × att[attacking team] × dfn[defending team] × venue
μ is the division average per team per match (~1.5–1.6 goals, ~12.5 shots). venue is h at home (~1.10 for goals, ~1.07–1.08 for shots), 1 ÷ h away, and 1.0 at a neutral site.

How to read the factors: a defence with dfn = 0.80 allows 20% fewer goals than an average defence; a defence with dfn = 1.25 allows 25% more. An attack with att = 1.30 scores 30% more than average. The model updates all teams over 40 passes (with a light prior of 6 goals and 40 shots pulling tiny samples toward 1.0), so every team's rating automatically accounts for the strength of the opponents it faced.

SHOOTING AND SCORING: THE MATH STEP BY STEP

Forwards and midfielders are evaluated on their own non-penalty goals and shots per 90 minutes. Assists and earlier passes are not included. Here are the four steps from raw box scores to a tier:

  1. Strip penalties and adjust each match for opponent and venue

    Penalty kicks (both goals and attempts, read from the play-by-play and cross-checked against the season stats page) are removed so designated penalty takers are not boosted over teammates who win the fouls. Then each match's non-penalty goals ( g) and shots (s) are divided by how generous that opponent and venue were:

    match adjG = g ÷ (dfn_goals[opponent] × venue_goals)
    match adjS = s ÷ (dfn_shots[opponent] × venue_shots)
    Example: scoring 1 goal away (venue ≈ 0.91) against a stingy 0.80 defence divides by 0.73, giving 1.37 adjusted goals. Scoring 1 goal at home (venue ≈ 1.10) against a 1.30 defence divides by 1.43, giving 0.70 adjusted goals.

    Touchline sums adjG and adjS across every counted match of the season.

  2. Combine adjusted goals and shots into a per-90 “threat” rate

    Both totals are put on a per-90-minute basis and combined with fixed weights. Shots carry substantial weight alongside goals because goals alone swing widely on luck and finishing streaks, while shot volume is steadier and predicts future scoring better than past goals do.

    threat = wG × (adjG × 90 ÷ minutes) + wS × (adjS × 90 ÷ minutes)
    Division Goal weight (wG) Shot weight (wS) 1 goal equals
    Men 0.234 0.0885 2.64 shots
    Women 0.238 0.0749 3.18 shots

  3. Blend with last season (if eligible) and the position average

    Even a full season has random streaks. To prevent a hot month from overstating a player's level, Touchline blends the player's threat rate with the position-group average (the mean threat of all complete players in that position group and gender with at least 600 minutes that season). If the player also logged 600+ minutes as a forward or midfielder at the same programme the season before, that earlier season is blended in too — and the pull toward average shrinks because there is twice as much evidence:

    Seasons counted Men's blend Women's blend
    1 season 79% this season + 21% group avg 82% this season + 18% group avg
    2 seasons 59% this + 34% last + 7% group avg 64% this + 30% last + 6% group avg

  4. Draw the cut lines inside each position group

    All eligible players in the position group (forwards separately from midfielders) are ordered by their blended estimate:

    • Tier 1: the top 5% (rounded up, plus any exact ties at the cutoff).
    • Tier 2: the next 15% — everyone else in the top 20% (rounded up, plus ties).
    • Eligible, no tier: the remaining ~80%.

    Once the tiers are assigned, the underlying estimates and ranks are discarded.

Worked example · Illustrative numbers (men's forward)

Suppose a men's forward plays 1,350 minutes (15 full matches), recording 9 non-penalty goals and 42 non-penalty shots against slightly tougher-than-average defences, and the men's forward average threat is 0.2800.

  • 1. Opponent-adjusted 9.75 adjG and 45.0 adjS → 0.65 adjG/90 and 3.00 adjS/90
  • 2. Season threat 0.234 × 0.65 + 0.0885 × 3.00 = 0.1521 + 0.2655 = 0.4176
  • 3a. If 1 season counted 0.79 × 0.4176 + 0.21 × 0.2800 = 0.389
  • 3b. If 2 seasons counted With last season's threat at 0.3900: 0.59 × 0.4176 + 0.34 × 0.3900 + 0.07 × 0.2800 = 0.399
  • 4. Applying the cut Among 320 eligible men's forwards, the top 16 (5%) receive Tier 1 and the next 48 (to 20%) receive Tier 2.

TEAM DEFENCE: THE MATH STEP BY STEP

A box score says who took a shot, not who broke up a counterattack or held the offside line. Because box scores cannot split defending among individual players, the Team defence tier belongs to the whole team — every regular defender and goalkeeper with the same counted seasons at a programme always shares the same result.

  1. Add up goals conceded against what opponents usually score

    Across every collected match with complete goals and shots (a team needs at least 12 such matches to qualify), Touchline totals three numbers for the team:

    • GA: total goals the team conceded.
    • SA: total shots the team faced.
    • EGA (expected goals against): for each match, how many goals that opponent would score against an average defence at that venue ( μ × att[opponent] ÷ venue from the goals model), summed across the season. On a player sheet this is printed as “what its opponents usually score.”

  2. Sort teams into three equal workload groups

    Touchline computes each qualifying team's shots faced per match (SA ÷ matches) and divides the division into three equal thirds using the 1/3 and 2/3 quantiles (a team landing right on a boundary joins the heavier group):

    • Light workload: bottom third of shots faced per match.
    • Typical workload: middle third.
    • Heavy workload: top third.

    Teams are compared only inside their own workload group, so a back line on a dominant possession team is not rewarded merely for facing very few shots.

  3. Measure goals prevented as a log ratio

    Rather than raw goal difference, Touchline compares goals conceded to expected goals as a ratio on a logarithmic scale:

    team record = −ln( max(GA, 0.5) ÷ EGA ) = ln( EGA ÷ max(GA, 0.5) )
    Giving up exactly what opponents usually score gives 0. Allowing fewer goals is positive (better); allowing more is negative (worse). Cutting expected goals in half — 10 instead of 20, or 15 instead of 30 — gives the exact same record (+0.693). The 0.5 floor simply avoids dividing by zero if a team concedes 0 goals.

  4. Standardise inside the workload group

    Inside each workload group (Light, Typical or Heavy) for that season, Touchline measures how far each team's record stands above or below its peers in standard deviations (a z-score):

    season score = (team record − workload group mean) ÷ workload group standard deviation
    This puts Light, Typical and Heavy seasons onto one shared scale, so a player's seasons can be averaged cleanly even if their team's workload changes from one year to the next.

  5. Average over 2 or 3 consecutive seasons and take the top 20%

    Because a single season of goals allowed is noisy, a player must be a regular in two or three consecutive seasons at the same programme and position group. The player's multi-season score is the plain average of the team's season scores across those two or three seasons. All eligible defenders and goalkeepers are pooled together (men and women separately), and the top 20% (rounded up, plus ties) hold the Team defence tier.

Worked example · Illustrative numbers (two-season defender)

Suppose a defender is a regular at the same programme for two consecutive seasons, and her team moves from a heavy workload last year to a typical workload this year.

  • Last season (Heavy) Gave up 24 goals vs. 32.0 expected → record = ln(32 ÷ 24) = +0.288. Heavy group mean −0.062, sd 0.250 → season score = (0.288 − (−0.062)) ÷ 0.250 = +1.40
  • This season (Typical) Gave up 16 goals vs. 24.0 expected → record = ln(24 ÷ 16) = +0.405. Typical group mean +0.075, sd 0.300 → season score = (0.405 − 0.075) ÷ 0.300 = +1.10
  • Two-season average (1.40 + 1.10) ÷ 2 = +1.25
  • Applying the cut If +1.25 lands in the top 20% of all eligible defenders and goalkeepers, she — and every teammate in defence or goal with those same two seasons — holds the Team defence tier.

Notice why the heavy-workload season (+1.40) outscored the typical-workload season (+1.10) even though the team conceded 8 more goals: teams facing heavy shot volume usually concede more than expected, so holding opponents 25% below expectation under heavy fire stands out further from its peers.

THE TEAM FIGURES ON A SHEET

Every regular defender's and goalkeeper's player sheet prints three team figures for the current season, whether or not the player holds a tier:

  1. Shots faced a match, with the workload group (beside the division average). Shows which third of teams this defence is compared against.
  2. Shots faced that went in (beside the division average). The percentage of shots against that became goals.
  3. Goals given up, beside what the opponents usually score. Printed as a single sentence — for example, “Gave up 24 goals where its opponents usually score 31.” This is the GA vs. EGA comparison the team defence tier is built on.

WHAT EACH STATUS ON A SHEET MEANS

Every player at a Division II programme Touchline follows who appears in a box score gets one of these five statuses:

  • A tier, with its kind — such as Tier 1 · Shooting and scoring, Tier 2 · Shooting and scoring or Team defence tier.
  • Eligible, no tier — cleared the playing-time and season rules and was compared, but finished outside the top 20% (about four in five eligible players).
  • Needs more minutes — did not reach the minutes (or team-minute share) threshold. Nothing else is implied.
  • Needs a second season — a regular defender or goalkeeper this season, but not a regular at this programme the season before.
  • Incomplete records — could not be compared fairly, with the specific reason printed on the sheet: minutes missing from too many box scores (with no stats total), no position on the roster or in box scores, a name that could not be reliably matched across matches, or (for defenders and goalkeepers) fewer than 12 collected team matches.

WHAT BOX SCORES CANNOT SEE

Box scores do not record where on the pitch a player operated, who won a tackle, who started a buildup pass, or how a player performed in earlier seasons at a different programme. They also list only who played — never why someone did not play (injury, rotation or rest), and Touchline never states or implies a reason.

HOW STEADY A TIER IS

Any cutoff has close calls: a single goal, a handful of shots, or being listed as a midfielder rather than a forward can move a player sitting near the 5% or 20% line.

To measure how much of a tier reflects real performance rather than luck, Touchline splits a season's matches into two random halves and runs the entire formula independently on each half. Because 20% of eligible players receive a tier, pure luck would produce about 2 in 10 repeat holders across halves. Instead:

  • Tier 1 (shooting and scoring): about 8 or 9 in 10 hold a tier on the other half (about 7 in 10 among men's forwards).
  • Tier 2 (shooting and scoring): about 5 in 10 hold a tier on the other half.
  • Team defence: about 4 in 10 hold it on the other half of a single season — goals conceded are noisier than shots taken, which is why the tier requires two or three seasons in a row. Among holders who are regulars again the following season, about 7 in 10 hold it again (partly because their multi-season windows overlap).

A full season uses twice as many matches as either half, so the published tiers are steadier than these half-season numbers.

WHEN TIERS ARE SET

Once a season, in November, before postseason honours are announced, and not moved afterward. The minutes printed beside a tier are the minutes counted through that day. Tiers for the seasons before 2026 were worked out in November 2026 by the same rule, from every match of those seasons, postseason included. The first season with tiers is 2022, and nothing before it is counted: no 2022 defender or goalkeeper could hold the team defence tier, and a 2022 forward or midfielder is judged on that season alone.
